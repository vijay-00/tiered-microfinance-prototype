Tiered Microfinance Prototype · Design brief · Whole app · v1

Every screen, ready for a modern design

The prototype works end to end. A member applies; the society pools ten to twenty approved loans; the federation sanctions a wholesale tranche; members repay every month; and every payment is split between the federation and the society, in double-entry books. Every screen was built for correctness, not for looks. This brief walks through the whole app so it can be given one modern, coherent design. For each screen it covers where it is, who uses it, what it is for, the flow, what is on it today and what we need designed. We will rebuild each screen to your design. No behaviour, figure or rule changes.

Prepared 29/10/2026. Screenshots come from the local build at 1440px (desktop) and 768px (tablet), on the demo date, Thursday 29 October 2026. All data is mock and every name is fictional. Unless a caption says otherwise, the view is the secretary of Annapurna Credit Society in scenario A, the clean run. Scenarios B (delinquency) and C (prepayment and recovery) appear where they show a state that A cannot.

The product in one paragraph Pragati Apex Credit Federation (PACF) lends wholesale to its member credit societies at 9.5% a year. A society, such as Annapurna, Gramodaya or Sahakar Mitra in Karnataka, takes applications from its members and pools ten to twenty approved loans into one funding request. Once the federation sanctions it, the society draws the tranche and lends to each member at 11.5%. Members repay a fixed monthly instalment (EMI). Each payment is split: wholesale interest and all the principal go on to the federation at the next monthly remittance, and the 2.0% spread stays with the society as its margin. Late members cost the society cash, not margin. Principal held back from the federation costs margin for good.
Who sees what
  • Society Secretary: everything in their society. Decides loans above ₹50,000, builds and submits pools, draws down, disburses and remits.
  • Loan Officer: takes and appraises applications, decides loans up to ₹50,000, and records payments.
  • Auditor: every society screen, read only.
  • Apex Officer (the federation): exposure across societies, funding requests, review and sanction, and society records.
  • Member: their own loans, schedule and payments, at the retail rate only. Nothing about wholesale pricing or margin reaches them.
Keep, whatever the look
  • Money in rupees with Indian grouping and two decimals (₹5,35,169.01), tabular figures, right-aligned in tables. Negative amounts look negative. Rates read “11.5% a year”; dates “29 Oct 2026”.
  • Every amount that is a link stays a link. Any dashboard figure opens the journal lines behind it in at most two clicks.
  • Words that explain numbers are content, not decoration: the margin explanation, the payment split preview, and the refusal messages the engine returns.
  • The demo bar must never look like part of the product. Today it is an amber strip.
  • Colour never carries meaning alone: every state also has a word or an icon.
  • Desktop first, working down to tablet width (768px). Keyboard and screen reader use: dialogs trap and return focus, a skip link leads to the page, and focus moves to each new page.
  • Light and dark. Both token sets exist today, and dark follows the system setting.
Built with React and Tailwind 4, with colour tokens in oklch; Radix for dialogs, menus and tabs; lucide icons; the system font. You are free to propose a typeface, an icon set, a palette, radii and elevation. We will express them as tokens, so every screen picks them up at once.

How the screens connect

ORIGINATION Apply (A2) → Appraise (A3) → Decide (A3, officer up to ₹50,000) → Pool (P2) → Submit to the federation → Begin review (E3) → Sanction or decline (E3) → Re-scope if sanctioned short (P3) → Draw down and disburse (T2)
SERVICING Instalments fall due (clock) → Record payments (K1) → Each payment split between federation and society (K1, L1) → Remit to the federation on the date (T2) → Margin kept (T2, R1)
BOOKS Every money movement posts a balanced journal entry → Dashboard figures (D1) open their journal lines (G1) → Statements (G2) and reports (R1) are folded from the same journal

Three ideas recur on almost every screen and should look the same everywhere:

  • The split. Any amount members pay divides into “to the federation” and “kept by the society”. It shows as a two-colour bar in the payment drawer (K1), the pool builder (P2), the pool economics (P3, E3) and the tranche margin (T2).
  • Theoretical against realised. What the schedules promised against what the books show, with the difference explained in words (D1, R1).
  • Traceable figures. A figure that is a link opens the journal lines that add up to it. Today this is a plain underline on hover. It needs a consistent, quiet affordance (F3).

The demo, in brief

An amber demo bar sits above every page (S2). It switches role and society, moves a simulated clock (+1 day, +1 month, next due date), loads one of three prepared scenarios, starts a guided walkthrough, and shows a green dot while all 36 accounting checks pass. Advancing the clock never posts anything; only people record payments and remittances.

S4Loading, empty, refused and not-found statesNew pattern

Every page · every read waits 150–400ms, like a real backend

  • Loading: grey pulsing tiles of the right count and shape.
  • Empty: a sentence in the card (“Nothing remitted yet.”, “No loan is in this bucket.”, “Every member is up to date.”).
  • Refused: the engine refuses anything a rule forbids and says why, in words; the dialog or form shows it in red above its buttons. A failure the screen does not handle shows as a red strip under the demo bar.
  • Not found: a header and a link back.
  • Not allowed: actions a role cannot take are hidden or replaced with a sentence (the auditor sees the desk, but “not record payments in this role”).
DESIGN

  • Success after an action: today the drawer turns into a receipt, dialogs simply close and the page refreshes. Is a toast wanted?

Each entry expands to its debit and credit lines. T-accounts: one account as a classic T, balance brought down and carried down. Trial balance: each account in its debit or credit column at any date, with a Balanced pill. CSV on every view.

DESIGN
  • The trace card is how every dashboard figure is explained. It should feel like an answer, not a filter.
  • Filters (account, dates, source, search) in one bar; a date-range control.
  • The T-account in a modern idiom that accountants still recognise.

The reasons: members behind, members catching up, prepayment timing, wholesale interest above schedule (lost for good), a remittance outstanding, and rounding. Then month by month.
  • Ageing: the six buckets as tiles that filter the loan list; the total ties to the books.

Questions for the whole app

Q1Table densityQuestion

Most screens are tables of 5 to 10 columns, with two lines in the first cell (name and an id or place). Staff read them all day. Compact rows (as now, about 40px) or comfortable (52px)? A density switch? Sticky headers for long tables (schedules, journal)?

Q2PhoneQuestion

Society and federation screens are designed for desktop and must work at tablet width. The member screens (U1, U2) will mostly be seen on phones once a member portal exists, an open question in the technical design. Should they get a phone layout now?

Q3LanguagesQuestion

Every label is English today. The societies are in Karnataka, so Kannada will follow, and Kannada labels run 30–50% longer. Should buttons, tabs and tile labels be designed with that room now?

Not a design question: noticed while preparing this brief
  • Internal ids (loan_001, app_070) appear to staff beside names. See F3 for whether to keep them.
  • The demo bar wraps to two rows at 1440px since the society switcher and walkthrough menu were added (S2).
  • Forms use native selects for member, tenure, purpose, mode and filters (F1).
  • CSV downloads are built but not styled beyond an outline button.
